Theme 2

Aquatic & Terrestrial Carbon Cycling

Photochemical oxidation is a key yet poorly constrained process governing the degradation and modification of OM in surface environments. Its contribution to global carbon cycling remains uncertain, partly due to the lack of robust approaches for differentiating among competing oxidation pathways. By applying my isotopic method to aqueous phase OM oxidation experiments, I have found that different aqueous oxidants (i.e., O2, •OH, H2O2) impart distinct oxygen isotope signatures to the OM that can be used as a fingerprint for tracing these oxidation pathways (e.g., Crocker+ 2025, ACS Earth Space Chem.). My ongoing work aims to comprehensively characterize the oxidant-specific oxygen isotope signatures acquired through OM oxidation. The next phase of my research will apply this isotopic tracer directly to samples from natural waters to determine OM oxidation rates and mechanisms. Ultimately, this research will advance our understanding of how OM oxidation processes influence aquatic carbon fluxes, atmospheric exchange, and climate feedbacks, informing global carbon cycle models.

Theme 3

Atmospheric Organic Aerosol Chemistry

My research uses triple oxygen isotopes to trace the oxidation pathways that govern the formation and evolution of atmospheric organic aerosols. My recent work demonstrates that oxidant-specific oxygen isotope signatures are retained in atmospheric organic aerosols, providing a quantitative record of their oxidation history.
