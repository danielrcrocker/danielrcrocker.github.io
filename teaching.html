Teaching & Mentoring

Teaching, mentorship & science communication

Across the classroom and laboratory, my goal is to equip students with the confidence, quantitative skills, and scientific independence to ask difficult questions, evaluate evidence critically, and contribute creatively to Earth and environmental science.

Teaching

Connecting chemistry across scales

My goal as an educator is to teach students to use fundamental scientific principles to address real environmental and planetary problems. I am particularly passionate about undergraduate education because it provides a formative opportunity to shape how students engage with science. I design student-centered learning experiences that move beyond memorization by asking students to analyze evidence, develop hypotheses, and apply chemical and geochemical concepts to problems ranging from air pollution and climate change to planetary evolution. My educational approach has been developed through formal training in college-level pedagogy and refined through my undergraduate teaching experiences, which include serving as Instructor of Record for General Chemistry and teaching 25 courses spanning several chemical disciplines.

Courses of interest: General Chemistry, Environmental Chemistry, Atmospheric Chemistry, Isotope Geochemistry, Biogeochemical Cycles, and Cosmochemistry/Origins of Life.

Mentoring

Supporting developing scientists

Equally important to students' scientific development is engaged mentorship in a research setting. I view mentorship as a progression toward scientific independence, tailoring my approach to each student's experience and career goals while helping them develop the skills and confidence to formulate questions, design experiments, interpret results, and communicate their science. Using this mentorship model, I have directly mentored ten undergraduate researchers throughout my academic career, most of whom subsequently pursued graduate study.

GEMS — Geosciences Education & Mentorship Support

In addition to research mentorship, I participate annually as a mentor in the GEMS program, supporting students as they navigate graduate education, applications, fellowships, and professional development.

Science Communication & Outreach

Harvard Science Education Partners

Through the Harvard Museums of Science & Culture Science Education Partners program, I participate in virtual classroom visits that connect grade-school students directly with working scientists. These conversations give me an opportunity to introduce students to my research and discuss how scientific questions are developed and investigated.
